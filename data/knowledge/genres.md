# Genre guide

Each section maps everyday words to the exact genre labels used in the TuneScout catalog (data/songs.csv). Energy and valence are on a 0.0 to 1.0 scale. Valence means how bright or positive a song sounds.

## Genre: pop
Catalog genre label: pop
Also called: mainstream, top 40, chart music, radio hits, catchy songs.
Sound: catchy hooks, polished production, verse-chorus structure.
Typical values: energy 0.7 to 0.95, valence 0.7 to 0.9, low acousticness (produced sound).
Close relatives in the catalog: indie pop, edm, r&b.

## Genre: indie pop
Catalog genre label: indie pop
Also called: indie, bedroom pop, alt-pop, indie rock with pop hooks.
Sound: pop melodies with a looser, more handmade feel than mainstream pop.
Typical values: energy 0.6 to 0.8, valence 0.6 to 0.85, medium-low acousticness.
Close relatives in the catalog: pop, folk, rock.
Note: "indie pop" is a separate label from "pop"; asking for "pop" does not match it exactly.

## Genre: lofi
Catalog genre label: lofi
Also called: lo-fi, lo-fi hip hop, chillhop, study beats, beats to relax or study to.
Sound: slow, mellow beats with warm, slightly dusty textures and soft piano or guitar samples.
Typical values: energy 0.3 to 0.45, valence 0.5 to 0.65, fairly acoustic (0.7 to 0.9).
Close relatives in the catalog: jazz, ambient, hip-hop.

## Genre: rock
Catalog genre label: rock
Also called: guitar music, alternative, alt-rock, hard rock, classic rock, punk (closest match).
Sound: driving electric guitars, live drums, strong vocals.
Typical values: energy 0.75 to 0.95, valence 0.4 to 0.7, low acousticness.
Close relatives in the catalog: metal, indie pop.

## Genre: metal
Catalog genre label: metal
Also called: heavy metal, thrash, metalcore, headbanging music.
Sound: heavily distorted guitars, fast drums, aggressive vocals.
Typical values: energy 0.9 to 1.0, valence 0.1 to 0.35, very low acousticness.
Close relatives in the catalog: rock.
Note: the catalog has only one metal song, and it is very high energy.

## Genre: edm
Catalog genre label: edm
Also called: electronic dance music, house, techno, trance, rave music, club music, dubstep.
Sound: synthesizers, heavy bass drops, steady four-on-the-floor dance beats.
Typical values: energy 0.85 to 1.0, valence 0.6 to 0.9, almost no acousticness.
Close relatives in the catalog: pop, synthwave.

## Genre: synthwave
Catalog genre label: synthwave
Also called: retrowave, outrun, 80s synth, night-drive music, vaporwave (closest match).
Sound: retro 1980s synthesizers, gated drums, neon nighttime atmosphere.
Typical values: energy 0.6 to 0.8, valence 0.4 to 0.6, low acousticness.
Close relatives in the catalog: edm, pop.

## Genre: hip-hop
Catalog genre label: hip-hop
Also called: hip hop, rap, trap, boom bap, beats with rapping.
Sound: rhythmic vocals over beats, strong bass and drums.
Typical values: energy 0.65 to 0.85, valence 0.4 to 0.7, low acousticness.
Close relatives in the catalog: r&b, lofi.

## Genre: r&b
Catalog genre label: r&b
Also called: rnb, rhythm and blues, soul, neo-soul, slow jams.
Sound: smooth vocals, groove-based rhythms, warm bass.
Typical values: energy 0.45 to 0.65, valence 0.55 to 0.75, medium acousticness.
Close relatives in the catalog: hip-hop, jazz, pop.

## Genre: jazz
Catalog genre label: jazz
Also called: smooth jazz, coffee shop jazz, bossa nova, swing, blues (closest match).
Sound: acoustic instruments such as piano, upright bass, saxophone and brushed drums; improvisation.
Typical values: energy 0.3 to 0.5, valence 0.6 to 0.8, highly acoustic.
Close relatives in the catalog: lofi, r&b, classical.

## Genre: classical
Catalog genre label: classical
Also called: orchestral, piano music, instrumental, symphony, sonata, film score (closest match).
Sound: orchestral or solo instruments, no beats, wide dynamics.
Typical values: energy 0.1 to 0.35, valence 0.3 to 0.6, very acoustic.
Close relatives in the catalog: ambient, folk.

## Genre: ambient
Catalog genre label: ambient
Also called: atmospheric, drone, soundscape, space music, sleep music, new age.
Sound: slow evolving textures with little or no beat.
Typical values: energy 0.15 to 0.35, valence 0.5 to 0.7, very acoustic or soft.
Close relatives in the catalog: classical, lofi.

## Genre: folk
Catalog genre label: folk
Also called: acoustic, singer-songwriter, unplugged, americana.
Sound: acoustic guitar, simple arrangements, storytelling lyrics.
Typical values: energy 0.2 to 0.45, valence 0.25 to 0.6, very acoustic.
Close relatives in the catalog: country, classical, indie pop.

## Genre: country
Catalog genre label: country
Also called: country music, bluegrass, honky-tonk, western, americana.
Sound: acoustic and steel guitars, fiddle, storytelling vocals.
Typical values: energy 0.45 to 0.65, valence 0.55 to 0.75, fairly acoustic.
Close relatives in the catalog: folk.

## Genre: reggae
Catalog genre label: reggae
Also called: roots reggae, dub, island music, ska (closest match), dancehall (closest match).
Sound: offbeat guitar skank, relaxed groove, prominent bass.
Typical values: energy 0.5 to 0.65, valence 0.75 to 0.95, medium acousticness.
Close relatives in the catalog: r&b, pop.

## Genres that are not in the catalog
The catalog has no k-pop, latin, reggaeton, gospel, punk, blues, soul, techno or house labels. Use the closest catalog genre instead: k-pop to pop; latin and reggaeton to pop or reggae; gospel and soul to r&b; punk to rock; blues to jazz; techno and house to edm. Tell the user the exact genre is not available.
